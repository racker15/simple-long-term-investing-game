# Fifty-scenario editorial review

## What was reviewed

The library has 50 unique starting dates, evenly split between important and preregistered random dates. Every date has seven investment choices and 60 subsequent monthly observations per choice. The date draw and starting commits remain the selection record; no date or company was replaced because its later result was inconvenient.

The first post-reveal summaries were too thin: most new ones were around 40–60 words, below the implementation plan's 150–250-word narrative target. The revised narratives use short paragraphs to combine concrete investment comparisons, dated historical context and one or two useful questions. They explain ideas such as a stock split, dividends, buying price, recovery from a loss and the difference between a business result and a share return. Research jargon was removed from the six pilot event-card sets. Their starting bundles and all numerical return paths are preserved.

The text avoids suggesting that every headline is irrelevant. Selected stories now follow concrete business developments where useful: Windows 95, the iPod announcement, Microsoft's proposed legal settlement, and Tesla's reported deliveries. These developments are context, not claims that one announcement mathematically caused a whole five-year return.

## Collection patterns

These counts describe the fixed collection, not targets used to curate it:

| Observation                                        |     Count |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------: |
| An individual company is the highest-return choice |  40 of 50 |
| International share basket leads                   |   4 of 50 |
| US share basket leads                              |   3 of 50 |
| Bond choice leads                                  |   3 of 50 |
| Individual-company paths ending below their start  | 31 of 150 |
| Distinct featured stock symbols                    |        38 |

Among important dates, a company leads 19 of 25 times; among random dates, 21 of 25. There are three company choices competing with each individual broad-asset choice, so these winner counts are not a fair head-to-head test of stock picking versus diversification. They also compare hindsight's best choice, rather than a player's ability to pick it beforehand. The game must not present these counts as evidence that a player should concentrate money in a company.

Companies recur: Apple appears in 24 scenarios, IBM in 18, Microsoft in 16 and Disney in 15. Dates and five-year windows also overlap. The scenarios are therefore not 50 independent experiments or a representative sample of all listed companies. Contemporary salience and available reconstructable histories shape the company list. A reader's familiarity with companies that survived is a further limitation. These facts are disclosed rather than “fixed” by discarding valid dates or replacing companies after looking at returns.

## Boundaries and remaining care

- Dollar gains are not inflation-adjusted buying-power gains.
- The broad international choice represents developed markets outside the US, not every foreign country.
- Adjusted-price stock histories are educational total-return approximations. Material corporate-event exceptions remain in provenance, including AIG's warrant-distribution treatment.
- Professional forecasts remain dated expectations. The prose does not score a forecast as foolish merely because events later changed.
- A positive five-year result can include severe intermediate losses. A five-year loss is also retained without being converted into a recovery story.
- January 2020 knows about a coronavirus emergency; March 2020 knows about the pandemic. Later vaccine announcements stay in the reveal.
- Starting information remains locked. This pass edits post-reveal explanations, not the information from which the original choices were made.

The existing browser walkthrough was extended to every date on desktop and mobile. Exact-head results and the initial transient screenshot failure are recorded in `expansion-progress.md`. The normal application entry remains the fictional demonstration; the historical library is exercised through the development preview. No deployment or production-session enablement is implied.
